# Tesla EV Copy Guidelines (Fictional)

> **Not an official Tesla document.** This guide was written by students for the APAN 5560
> class project "Copywriting Assistant, EV Marketer". The voice rules, word lists and channel
> limits below are invented for the project. Tesla does not endorse them.

Scope: ad copy for three vehicles — **Model 3**, **Model Y** and **Cybertruck** — in the US market.

---

## 1. Brand Voice

We sound like an engineer who is proud of the product and respects the reader's time:
short, exact and quietly confident. Three traits guide every line.

### 1.1 Direct
- Lead with the product, not with a mood. The first words name the car or what it does.
- Cut every word that does not add information. Fewer adjectives, more verbs.
- Do: "Model Y charges while you grab lunch."
- Don't: "Imagine a world where your journey becomes a seamless symphony of electric possibility."

### 1.2 Precise
- Prefer a specific fact over a general claim. If there is no fact, say less.
- Explain a feature by what it does, in one sentence, using the manual's own terms.
- Do: "Software updates arrive over Wi-Fi, so new features install while the car is parked."
- Don't: "Cutting-edge technology that keeps getting better in amazing ways."

### 1.3 Understated
- Let the product carry the excitement. No hype words, no stacked superlatives.
- Dry, light wit is allowed in one line per piece. Never sarcasm aimed at people or other brands.
- No exclamation marks in headlines; at most one in the whole piece.
- Do: "Room for seven. Or one very large dog."
- Don't: "The ULTIMATE family SUV that will BLOW YOUR MIND!!"

## 2. Audience

| Segment | What they want to hear | Typical model |
|---|---|---|
| Efficiency-minded commuter, first EV | Low running costs, simple charging, a car that improves with updates | Model 3 |
| Family and everyday utility | Space, safety, easy road trips on the Supercharger network | Model Y |
| Work and outdoor utility | Towing, cargo, durability, power on site | Cybertruck |

Assume the reader is curious and comfortable with technology but not an engineer.

## 3. Vocabulary

### 3.1 Preferred terms

| Use | Instead of | Why |
|---|---|---|
| charge, charging | fuel up, refuel | EVs do not use fuel |
| Supercharger, Supercharging | fast-charge station, gas station | Our own network name; capitalize it |
| Wall Connector (home charging) | home charger box, EV plug | Product name used in the manual |
| estimated range | guaranteed range, real range | Range depends on conditions |
| zero tailpipe emissions | zero emissions, emission-free | Electricity generation can emit |
| software updates | auto-upgrades, magic updates | The manual's term |
| Traffic-Aware Cruise Control, Autosteer, Full Self-Driving (Supervised) | Autopilot, self-driving, autonomous, driverless | The 2026 manuals use these names only; see 3.2 |
| Trip Planner | route planner, charging map | Plans Supercharger stops; name used in the manual |
| Precondition, Scheduled Precondition | warm-up mode, battery conditioning | The manual's term |
| Sentry Mode, Dashcam | security camera mode, surveillance | Feature names used in the manual |
| Keep Climate On, Pet Mode, Camp Mode | dog mode, utility mode | Feature names used in the manual |
| Tesla mobile app, phone key | Tesla app key, digital key | Names used in the manual |
| Trailer Mode | tow mode, trailering | The manual's term |
| front trunk (frunk) | eTrunk, front storage | The manual's term |

### 3.2 Model and feature names
- Write **Model 3**, **Model Y**, **Cybertruck** exactly like this. Never "Tesla 3" or "Cyber Truck".
- Spec database names are the same: `Model 3`, `Model Y`, `Cybertruck`.
- **Full Self-Driving** always carries **(Supervised)** on first mention in a piece. Never abbreviate to "FSD" in ad copy.
- Any line that mentions Autosteer or Full Self-Driving (Supervised) must also say the driver stays
  in control, for example "Requires active driver supervision." Autosteer is a hands-on feature.
- The Cybertruck manual has no Autosteer. Cybertruck driver assistance is Traffic-Aware Cruise Control
  and Full Self-Driving (Supervised) only.
- Features the manual marks as depending on region, hardware or software need "available" in copy.

### 3.3 Banned words and phrases
The off-brand checker flags these. Do not use them in any channel.

- revolutionary, game-changer, game-changing, disruptive, mind-blowing
- best-in-class, #1, number one, unbeatable — unless a cited source is attached
- self-driving, autonomous, driverless, hands-free, robotaxi, "drives itself"
- Autopilot (not used in the 2026 manuals; name the actual feature instead)
- "Full Self-Driving" without "(Supervised)", and "FSD"
- bulletproof, indestructible, unbreakable
- never worry, worry-free, unlimited, endless range
- zero emissions, emission-free, 100% green, eco-friendly (vague)
- free Supercharging, unlimited Supercharging, cheapest — unless a dated offer is supplied
- range anxiety (we describe the solution, not the fear)
- competitor brand or model names
- other brands' feature names: Super Cruise, One-Pedal Driving, Smart Cruise Control, i-PEDAL, V2L, PowerBase, Bluelink, OnStar

## 4. Claims and Numbers

1. **Every number comes from the spec database.** Range, battery capacity, charging power,
   0–60 time, top speed, price and warranty years are looked up, never written from memory.
2. **How a feature works comes from the owner's manual.** Charging steps, regenerative braking,
   Autosteer, Traffic-Aware Cruise Control, towing, power outlets and Sentry Mode descriptions must match the manual for that model.
3. **Never combine a database number with a manual number** in the same claim. If they disagree,
   use the database number and drop the manual number.
4. Range is always "estimated". Add "Actual range varies with conditions and driving habits."
   to any copy that states a range.
5. Charging time claims must name the condition (Supercharger or Wall Connector, and battery
   start/end level). If the condition is unknown, describe charging without a time.
6. Price claims say "starting at" and need the model year.
7. Performance claims (0–60, top speed) must name the variant from the database.
8. No comparisons with named competitors. Compare with the reader's current routine instead.

## 5. Model Messaging

Themes to lead with. Facts supporting each theme must still follow section 4.

### Model 3
- Lead with: efficiency and a clean, simple driving experience.
- Story: the daily commute gets quieter and cheaper, and the car gains features through updates.
- Mood words: clean, quick, effortless.

### Model Y
- Lead with: everyday utility for a household.
- Story: school drop-off on Monday, a road trip on Saturday with Supercharger stops planned by the car.
- Mood words: roomy, practical, ready.

### Cybertruck
- Lead with: capability for work and the outdoors.
- Story: a truck that tows, hauls and powers tools on site, then drives home quietly.
- Mood words: tough, capable, unconventional.
- Durability claims describe materials or features named in the manual; never "bulletproof".

## 6. Channel Rules

### 6.1 Social ad (Instagram, Facebook, X)
- Primary text: 125 characters or fewer.
- Headline: 40 characters or fewer.
- One call to action: "Order now", "Schedule a demo drive" or "Learn more".
- At most two hashtags and one emoji. No hashtags in the headline.

### 6.2 Search and display ad
- Headline: 30 characters or fewer. Description: 90 characters or fewer.
- Put the model name in the headline.
- At most one number per ad, taken from the spec database.

### 6.3 Email
- Subject line: 50 characters or fewer, no all caps, no exclamation mark.
- Preheader: 90 characters or fewer, adds new information to the subject.
- Body: 120–200 words in three parts — a concrete moment, two or three supporting facts,
  one call to action.
- Close with the range disclaimer from section 4 if a range is mentioned, and the supervision
  line from section 3.2 if Autosteer or Full Self-Driving (Supervised) is mentioned.

## 7. Examples

Numbers below are placeholders (`{range_miles}`, `{acceleration_0_60_mph}`) filled from the spec database.

### 7.1 Social ad — Model Y
**On brand**
> Headline: Room for the whole weekend
> Text: Bikes, bags and the dog. Up to {range_miles} miles of estimated range. Schedule a demo drive.

Why it works: one concrete scene, one database number marked as estimated, one call to action.

**Off brand**
> Headline: The REVOLUTIONARY Self-Driving SUV!!
> Text: Never worry about range again. FSD drives you everywhere for free!

Flags: all caps, two exclamation marks, "revolutionary", "self-driving", "never worry", "FSD", "drives you", "free".

### 7.2 Search ad — Model 3
**On brand**
> Headline: Model 3: 0–60 in {acceleration_0_60_mph}s
> Description: Quiet, efficient and improved by software updates. Schedule a demo drive.

Note: the variant shown on the landing page must match the database row used for the number.

**Off brand**
> Headline: Best Sedan Ever Made
> Description: Beats every competitor with zero emissions and the cheapest price.

Flags: "best" without source, competitor comparison, "zero emissions", "cheapest".

### 7.3 Email — Cybertruck
**On brand**
> Subject: Your job site just got quieter
> Preheader: Tow, haul and power your tools, then drive home on electric.
> Body opens with an early morning on a job site, explains towing and on-site power in two
> sentences that match the owner's manual, states one database range figure with the
> disclaimer, and ends with "Learn more".
