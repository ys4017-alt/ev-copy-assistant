# Chevrolet EV Copy Guidelines (Fictional)

> **Not an official Chevrolet or GM document.** This guide was written by students for the
> APAN 5560 class project "Copywriting Assistant, EV Marketer". The voice rules, word lists and
> channel limits below are invented for the project. Chevrolet and GM do not endorse them.

Scope: ad copy for three EVs — **Equinox EV**, **Blazer EV** and **Silverado EV** — in the US market.

---

## 1. Brand Voice

We sound like a capable neighbor who has used the vehicle for real work and real weekends:
plainspoken, practical and upbeat. Three traits guide every line.

### 1.1 Plainspoken
- Use everyday American English. If a sentence needs a glossary, rewrite it.
- Say what the feature lets you do first, then name it.
- Do: "Ease off the pedal and the truck slows down on its own, sending energy back to the battery."
- Don't: "Proprietary energy recapture deceleration modulation enhances efficiency."

### 1.2 Practical
- Ground every line in a real job or a real trip: a job site, a hardware store run, a lake weekend.
- Show capability through use, not through adjectives.
- Do: "Plug your tools into the bed outlets and get the job done before lunch."
- Don't: "Unleash next-level electrified capability for the modern adventurer."

### 1.3 Upbeat
- Optimistic and friendly. Light humor about everyday life is fine; never about other brands.
- One exclamation mark is allowed in body copy. None in headlines or email subject lines.
- Do: "More room for gear. Fewer stops on the way to the trailhead."
- Don't: "Stop settling for boring cars like everyone else!"

## 2. Audience

| Segment | What they want to hear | Typical model |
|---|---|---|
| Practical family switching from gas | Everyday range, simple home charging, a familiar SUV feel | Equinox EV |
| Style-conscious driver who still needs space | A sporty look with room for people and cargo | Blazer EV |
| Contractor, rancher, outdoor enthusiast | Towing, payload, power outlets, durability | Silverado EV |

Assume the reader is practical and skeptical of hype. Many are switching from a gas vehicle and
want to know their routine will still work.

## 3. Vocabulary

### 3.1 Preferred terms

| Use | Instead of | Why |
|---|---|---|
| charge, charging | fuel up, refuel | EVs do not use fuel |
| DC fast charging | supercharging, rapid juice | "Supercharger" is a competitor's brand |
| Level 2 charging, portable charge cord | wall plug, fast home charger | Terms used in the manuals |
| charge schedule, Target Charge Level | charge limit, charge cap | Terms used in the manuals |
| Adaptive Cruise Control, Lane Keep Assist | smart cruise, lane assist, autosteer | Names used in the manuals |
| estimated range | guaranteed range, real range | Range depends on conditions |
| zero tailpipe emissions | zero emissions, emission-free | Electricity generation can emit |
| One-Pedal Driving, Regen on Demand | single-pedal mode, brake-free driving | Feature names used in the manuals |
| Super Cruise hands-free driving assistance on compatible roads | self-driving, autopilot, autonomous | See 3.2 |
| myChevrolet app, OnStar | the Chevy app, phone app | Names used in the manuals |
| power outlets, PowerBase | V2L, generator mode, power bank | Names used in the manuals |
| eTrunk, Midgate (Silverado EV) | frunk, pass-through | Names used in the Silverado EV manual |
| trailering, towing | hauling a trailer around | Both appear in the manuals |

### 3.2 Model and feature names
- Write **Equinox EV**, **Blazer EV**, **Silverado EV** exactly like this, with a space before "EV".
- Spec database names are the same: `Equinox EV`, `Blazer EV`, `Silverado EV`.
- **Super Cruise** is always described as "hands-free driving assistance" and always limited to
  "compatible roads" (the manuals say "Super Cruise mapped and enabled roads").
- Any line that mentions Super Cruise must also say the driver stays attentive, for example
  "Always pay attention to the road."
- Features marked "if equipped" in the manual (for example PowerBase, Midgate, Super Cruise)
  need "available" in copy: "available Super Cruise", "available Midgate".
- Super Cruise needs an active OnStar plan; say so when Super Cruise is the main message.
- Towing differs by model: the Blazer EV SS must not tow. Do not show the SS towing.

### 3.3 Banned words and phrases
The off-brand checker flags these. Do not use them in any channel.

- revolutionary, game-changer, game-changing, disruptive, next-level, unleash
- best-in-class, #1, number one, unbeatable — unless a cited source is attached
- self-driving, autonomous, driverless, autopilot, "drives itself"
- "hands-free" about driving without "Super Cruise" and "compatible roads" in the same sentence
- Supercharger, supercharging, Tesla, or any other competitor brand or model name
- other brands' feature names: Autosteer, Traffic-Aware Cruise Control, Sentry Mode, Smart Cruise Control, i-PEDAL, V2L, Bluelink
- indestructible, unbreakable, bulletproof
- never worry, worry-free, unlimited, endless range
- zero emissions, emission-free, 100% green, eco-friendly (vague)
- cheapest, lowest price, free charging — unless a dated offer is supplied
- range anxiety (we describe the solution, not the fear)

## 4. Claims and Numbers

1. **Every number comes from the spec database.** Range, battery capacity, charging power,
   0–60 time, towing figures, price and warranty years are looked up, never written from memory.
2. **How a feature works comes from the owner's manual.** Charging steps, One-Pedal Driving,
   Regen on Demand, Super Cruise, trailering, power outlets and PowerBase descriptions must match
   the manual for that model.
3. **Never combine a database number with a manual number** in the same claim. If they disagree,
   use the database number and drop the manual number.
4. Range is always "estimated". Add "Actual range varies with conditions and driving habits."
   to any copy that states a range.
5. Charging time claims must name the condition (DC fast charging or Level 2, and battery
   start/end level). If the condition is unknown, describe charging without a time.
6. Price claims say "starting at" and need the model year.
7. Towing claims must name the variant and say "when properly equipped".
8. No comparisons with named competitors. Compare with the reader's current gas vehicle routine instead.

## 5. Model Messaging

Themes to lead with. Facts supporting each theme must still follow section 4.

### Equinox EV
- Lead with: an easy switch from gas for everyday family life.
- Story: same school runs, same grocery trips, now charged at home overnight.
- Mood words: familiar, easy, dependable.

### Blazer EV
- Lead with: sporty looks that still fit real life.
- Story: a Friday night drive downtown and a Saturday soccer tournament, in the same vehicle.
- Mood words: bold, lively, versatile.

### Silverado EV
- Lead with: work capability with electric power on board.
- Story: tow the trailer, run tools from the bed outlets, and drive home without a gas stop.
- Mood words: strong, hardworking, ready.
- Mention the available Midgate only for trims the manual says are equipped.

## 6. Channel Rules

### 6.1 Social ad (Instagram, Facebook)
- Primary text: 125 characters or fewer.
- Headline: 40 characters or fewer.
- One call to action: "Build yours", "Find a dealer" or "See offers".
- At most two hashtags and one emoji. No hashtags in the headline.

### 6.2 Search and display ad
- Headline: 30 characters or fewer. Description: 90 characters or fewer.
- Put the model name in the headline.
- At most one number per ad, taken from the spec database.

### 6.3 Email
- Subject line: 50 characters or fewer, no all caps, no exclamation mark.
- Preheader: 90 characters or fewer, adds new information to the subject.
- Body: 120–200 words in three parts — a real-life moment, two or three supporting facts,
  one call to action.
- Close with the range disclaimer from section 4 if a range is mentioned, and the attention line
  from section 3.2 if Super Cruise is mentioned.

## 7. Examples

Numbers below are placeholders (`{range_miles}`) filled from the spec database.

### 7.1 Social ad — Equinox EV
**On brand**
> Headline: Same routine. No gas station.
> Text: School runs, practice, groceries, then plug in at home. Up to {range_miles} miles of estimated range. Build yours.

Why it works: a familiar routine, one database number marked as estimated, one call to action.

**Off brand**
> Headline: Unleash the REVOLUTIONARY Equinox EV!
> Text: Never worry about range. It basically drives itself on any road!

Flags: "unleash", "revolutionary", all caps, exclamation mark in headline, "never worry", "drives itself", no road limitation.

### 7.2 Search ad — Silverado EV
**On brand**
> Headline: Silverado EV: Power on Site
> Description: Run your tools from available bed outlets, then drive home electric. Find a dealer.

**Off brand**
> Headline: Toughest Truck, Beats Cybertruck
> Description: Indestructible, self-driving and the cheapest EV truck around.

Flags: competitor name, unsourced superlative, "indestructible", "self-driving", "cheapest".

### 7.3 Email — Blazer EV
**On brand**
> Subject: Weekend plans, meet Blazer EV
> Preheader: Room for the team, the gear and a stop for dinner on the way home.
> Body opens with a Saturday tournament drive, explains One-Pedal Driving in two sentences that
> match the owner's manual, states one database range figure with the disclaimer, and ends with
> "Build yours".
